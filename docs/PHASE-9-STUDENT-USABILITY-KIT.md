# Lugaish student usability sessions

Prepared kit only. No participants were recruited or tested during this implementation.

## Setup

Recruit 5–8 Arabic/English learners, including first-time and returning students and at least three who normally use a phone. Arrange valid test accounts with appropriate real test enrollments; include one without Paid Batch access. Use a dedicated test environment and content. Never provide production credentials or alter a real student's progress for testing.

Plan 30–40 minutes per participant. Obtain permission before recording the screen or microphone. Record anonymous participant IDs; do not include emails, private conversations, or passwords in observations. Explain that the product is being tested, not the student. They may pause or stop at any time.

Suggested introduction: “Please try these learning tasks as you normally would. Tell us what you expect and what feels unclear. I won't guide your clicks unless you're stuck. Your feedback helps us improve Lugaish.”

## Six tasks — give outcomes, not instructions

1. You want to learn Arabic or English. Find out what is available, what it costs and how you would begin. Explain what Paid Batch access means.
2. Sign in using your designated test account. Find an available lesson in your enrolled course and decide what to study next. If none is available, explain the message and your next action.
3. Open that lesson in Classroom, use its supported lesson content, switch a camera view, then return to My Classes without losing the intended lesson. Record any unavailable video/provider limitation instead of treating it as user failure.
4. Complete one supported practice activity, review any mistakes and find the next learning action. Identify whether results are actually saved. Use published validated course content, not the developer fixture, for this task.
5. Choose a Library seat, write a short note, start a focus session, switch views and tools, then find your note and timer again. Try rain if you want. Leave your seat when done.
6. In Speaking Practice, listen to a supplied phrase, record and replay one attempt, delete it and leave. Explain which feedback is available. If your account is eligible, also identify where a private group's chat is available; don't contact uninvolved students.

## Observation sheet

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P01 | 1 | Unassisted / assisted / failed / blocked | Seconds | None / hint / direct help | Exact observable event | Participant's words | P0–P3 |

Start timing when the task is read. Stop when the intended outcome is achieved or the student abandons it. Mark missing content, auth/service outages and device restrictions as **blocked**, not failed usability. Note the number of hints and the actual point where assistance was required. Use medians and counts, not invented composite scores.

Checklist: understands language/access; finds primary action; recognizes loading/error state; can recover; sees selected lesson; notices saved vs unsaved notes; retains timer through view changes; understands private chat scope; recognizes live microphone status; uses keyboard/touch comfortably; can leave and return; knows whether progress was actually saved.

## Short questionnaire

After each task: “How easy was that?” (1 very difficult–5 very easy), “What felt unclear?”, and “What did you expect to happen?”

At the end: “Which part would you use again?”, “Where did you lose confidence?”, “What one change would help most?”, “Could you tell which features were unavailable or restricted?”, “Was anything uncomfortable on your device?”

## Synthesis

Group repeated observations by task and root cause. Report unassisted/assisted/failed/blocked counts and median time separately. Include anonymized quotes only from actual consented sessions. Prioritize data loss/access problems first, then obstacles to lesson/study tasks. Keep participant feedback separate from automated QA results.
